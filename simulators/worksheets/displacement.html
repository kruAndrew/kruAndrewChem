Part A · Halogens and halide ions

  1. Use the results table and the ranking task to complete the two orders.
    Ease of reduction of the halogens (most → least)_______ > _______ > _______
    Ease of oxidation of the halide ions (most → least)_______ > _______ > _______
    What do you notice about the two orders?
  2. Complete the rule: "A halogen will react with a halide solution only if…"
  3. Write the general half-equation for a halogen acting as an oxidising agent (use X).
  4. Explain why the halogens become less easily reduced going down group 17. Use the ideas of atomic radius, shielding and attraction to the nucleus.
  5. Why is cyclohexane useful in these tests? Record the colours you will need.

Part B · Metals and metal ions

  1. Complete the sentences.
    In a metal / metal-ion reaction, the metal atom is _____________ and acts as the _____________ agent.
    The metal ion is _____________ and acts as the _____________ agent.
  2. Complete the rule: "A metal will react with a solution of metal ions only if…"
  3. How is the order of ease of reduction of the metal ions related to the order of ease of oxidation of the metals?
  4. Write one equation from the simulator where the electrons had to be balanced. Show how you balanced it, and give a quick check that you could use in an exam.

Part C · Interpreting data

You do not need to learn the order of the metals. In an exam, you will be given the data. Use the Unknown metals tab to practise.

  1. Write a short method for reading a metal / metal-ion results table. Explain how to find:
    • the most easily oxidised metal (strongest reducing agent)
    • the most easily reduced ion (strongest oxidising agent)
    • whether a reaction that has not been tested will happen
  2. Record your final ranking for one set of unknown metals. Explain how you decided which metal was at the top.
