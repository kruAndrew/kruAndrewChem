By the end you can: describe what happens to electrons in ionic, covalent and metallic bonding · use electronegativity (χ) to predict bonding type · link bonding type to structure and properties.

How to use it: click Species 1, choose an element, click Species 2, choose another, then press ▶ Bond!. Watch the Electron movement panel, then read the Bonding triangle panel and the Arrangement once bonded panel.

Part A · Ionic bonding: electrons are transferred

  1. Bond Na with Cl. Describe what happens to the outer electron of sodium.
  2. Complete the table for three ionic compounds. Use the formula the simulator gives, then explain it using the ion charges.

  3. Look at Arrangement once bonded for NaCl (press Spin). Describe the structure, and explain why NaCl has a high melting point.

Part B · Covalent bonding: electrons are shared

  1. Bond Cl with Cl, then H with Cl. Tick Show electron density in bonds. How is the shared pair different in the two molecules? Use the term partial charges (δ+ and δ−).
  2. Bond C with O, then Si with O. Both pairs share electrons, but the simulator shows very different structures. Describe the difference.

Part C · Metallic bonding: electrons are delocalized

  1. Bond Cu with Cu, then Cu with Zn. What happens to the outer electrons? Why can metals conduct electricity and be hammered into shape?
