Part D · Carbon Chameleon: one atom at a time

For each carbon: count +1 for every bond to a more electronegative atom (a double bond counts 2), −1 for every C–H bond, and 0 for C–C bonds.

  1. Ethanol is oxidised to ethanal and then to ethanoic acid. Give the oxidation state of each carbon atom.
    CH₃CH₂OHCH₃CHOCH₃COOH
    Oxidation state of C bonded to O
    Oxidation state of the CH₃ carbon
  2. Using your table, explain what "oxidation" means for this carbon, and why the formula method (average carbon) hides what is happening.

Part E · Boss Lab: averages vs individual atoms

  1. The formula method gives Fe in Fe₃O₄ an oxidation state of +8/3. Explain how this fraction arises when every real iron ion has a whole-number oxidation state. (Clue: Fe₃O₄ = FeO·Fe₂O₃.)

Part F · Formal Charge Lab (HL)

FC = V − N − ½B  (V = valence electrons, N = non-bonding electrons, B = bonding electrons)

  1. Draw the Lewis structure of CO (C≡O, one lone pair on each atom). Calculate the formal charge on each atom, then compare with their oxidation states.
  2. Oxidation state and formal charge both "share out" the bonding electrons. Explain how the two methods differ, and why neither gives a real charge.
  3. Exam-style. Two Lewis structures of N₂O are N=N=O and N≡N–O. Calculate the formal charge on each atom in both structures and deduce, with a reason, which structure is preferred. [3]
