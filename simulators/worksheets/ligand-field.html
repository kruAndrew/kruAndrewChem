By the end you can: describe the dative bond between a ligand and a metal ion · draw the splitting of the d orbitals in an octahedral complex · use the colour wheel to link the colour absorbed to the colour seen · explain how the ligand, the metal and its oxidation state affect Δo and the colour.

Part A · Copper(II) in water

Choose Cu, Cu²⁺ and H₂O. Have your data booklet open at the colour wheel (section 15).

  1. Write the formula of the complex ion, including its charge. State the coordination number.
  2. Look at the space-filling model. Which atom in H₂O donates the electrons? Describe the bond formed between each water molecule and the Cu²⁺ ion.
  3. Write the electron configuration of Cu²⁺. Then draw the d-orbital splitting diagram for the complex, showing the electrons. Label t2g, eg and Δo.
  4. Watch the animation. Describe what happens to an electron when the complex absorbs a photon of light.
  5. Use the waves and the colour wheel. Which colour of light is absorbed? What colour is the solution, and why?

Part D · Colourless ions

  1. Try Sc³⁺, Zn²⁺ and Cu⁺ with H₂O. Write the number of d electrons in each and explain why all three are colourless.

Part E · Putting numbers on it

  1. Choose Ti³⁺ and H₂O. Note the wavelength of the d–d band. Using E = hc/λ, calculate the energy of one photon at this wavelength, then the energy for one mole of photons in kJ mol⁻¹. Compare your answer with Δo in the readout.
  2. Exam-style. [Co(H₂O)₆]³⁺ is blue but [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is orange. Explain this difference. [3]
  3. Exam-style. Explain why aqueous solutions of zinc sulfate are colourless while aqueous solutions of copper(II) sulfate are blue. [3]
