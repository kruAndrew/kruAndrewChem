Formal charge: the other way of counting electrons

FC = V − N − ½B
  • V = valence electrons of the free atom  ·  N = non-bonding (lone-pair) electrons on the atom  ·  B = electrons in bonds to the atom
  • The formal charges in a structure add up to the overall charge of the species.
  • Preferred Lewis structure: formal charges as close to zero as possible; any negative FC on the most electronegative atom.

📘 Oxidation state rules

  1. Uncombined elements are 0 (Na, Cl₂, S₈, O₂).
  2. Monatomic ions: oxidation state = ion charge (Fe³⁺ → +3).
  3. Sum rule: oxidation states add up to 0 in a neutral compound, or to the ion charge in a polyatomic ion.
  4. Fluorine is always −1 (most electronegative element).
  5. Oxygen is usually −2. Exceptions: peroxides (O₂²⁻, −1), superoxides (O₂⁻, −½), bonded to F (OF₂, +2).
  6. Hydrogen is usually +1, but −1 in metal hydrides (NaH, LiAlH₄).
  7. Group 1 +1, Group 2 +2, Al +3 in compounds.
  8. Halogens are usually −1, but positive when bonded to O or to a more electronegative halogen (ClO⁻, ICl).
  9. Organic carbon, atom by atom: each bond to a more electronegative atom +1, each C–H −1, each C–C 0.
  10. Notation: oxidation states put the sign first (+6); ion charges put it last (2−). Names use Roman numerals: iron(III) oxide.
HL

Formal charge

FC = V − N − ½B
  • Lone pairs count fully to the atom; bonding pairs are split equally.
  • ΣFC = overall charge.
  • Prefer structures with FCs closest to zero, and negative FCs on the most electronegative atoms.
